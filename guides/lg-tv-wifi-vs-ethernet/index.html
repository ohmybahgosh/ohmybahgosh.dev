Wi-Fi or Ethernet on an LG TV?

On most devices a cable is the easy answer. LG TVs are different: the port is slower than good Wi-Fi.

Short answer. For regular streaming, either works. For big files, strong Wi-Fi often beats the 100 Mbit/s port, but a cable is steadier. On a rooted TV, LG-USB-2-ETH0 gives you both: a USB adapter as the wired connection at about 250 to 330 Mbit/s.

The numbers

connectionreal speed on the TV
Built-in Ethernet portabout 95 Mbit/s, every LG model measured
Wi-Fi 5 or 6, same room as the routeroften 200 Mbit/s or more
Wi-Fi through walls or floorsanywhere from great to unwatchable
USB adapter as eth0, rooted TVabout 250 to 330 Mbit/s

Test your own TV

Open the web browser

Go to a speed test site like fast.com on the TV.

Test on Wi-Fi, then on the cable

Same time of day, same spot. Run each two or three times.

Watch for steadiness, not just the top number

Wi-Fi that swings from 300 to 40 buffers more than a cable that sits at 95.

Which to pick

  • Netflix, YouTube, Disney+ and IPTV: a cable. 95 Mbit/s is plenty, and it never drops.
  • 4K remuxes from Plex or Jellyfin: Wi-Fi if it's strong and steady, or see Plex buffering on an LG TV.
  • Rooted TV: a USB adapter as eth0 is faster than both. LG-USB-2-ETH0.

FAQ

Is Wi-Fi faster than Ethernet on an LG TV?
Often, yes. The built-in port is 100 Mbit/s, about 95 in practice, and a strong Wi-Fi 5 or 6 signal can beat that. A cable is steadier, though.
Which should I use for 4K streaming?
Netflix and the other big apps need about 15 to 25 Mbit/s for 4K, so either works. For 4K remuxes from Plex or Jellyfin, Wi-Fi can beat the port, and a USB adapter as eth0 beats both.
Can I use Wi-Fi and Ethernet at the same time?
webOS uses one for traffic, and the cable wins when it's connected. On a rooted TV, LG-USB-2-ETH0 can keep Wi-Fi up next to a USB adapter, for waking the TV over the network.
