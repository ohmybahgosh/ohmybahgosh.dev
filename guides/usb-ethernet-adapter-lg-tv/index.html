Which USB Ethernet adapter for an LG TV?

Buy by chip, not by brand. An adapter only works if the TV's kernel has a driver for its chip, and LG's kernels vary by model and firmware.

Safe picks. A gigabit adapter with a Realtek RTL8153 or ASIX AX88179 chip. A Realtek RTL8156 2.5G adapter is fine too, but on a USB 2.0 port it's no faster.

The chips

chipon LG TVs
Realtek RTL8153 (gigabit), 0bda:8153The most common. Many kernels have Realtek's r8152 built in. Newer ones may only have the slower generic cdc_ncm driver, and a few none at all, where a driver built for that kernel can be loaded.
Realtek RTL8156 (2.5G), 0bda:8156Works on cdc_ncm out of the box, and faster with Realtek's newer r8152_oot driver where one is built for your kernel.
ASIX AX88179 (gigabit), 0b95:1790Works out of the box on many TVs with the kernel's own ax88179_178a driver. Where it's missing, there's often one to get or test.
ASIX AX88179A, AX88772DSkip for now: newer chips older TV kernels may not handle, and untested on a TV.
Aquantia AQC111/112, Davicom DM9601, Microchip LAN78xxNo LG kernel includes these drivers. Builds are waiting for testers, but a Realtek or ASIX adapter is the safer buy.

USB Ethernet adapter not working on an LG TV?

Three causes cover almost every case:

  • It's working, but as eth1. The adapter has a link and an address, yet settings say "Not Connected" and some apps won't play. webOS only treats eth0 as the wired connection. On a rooted TV, LG-USB-2-ETH0 fixes the name.
  • No driver for its chip. The adapter isn't detected at all, because the kernel on your model and firmware has no driver for that chip. LG-USB-2-ETH0's status shows adapters the kernel sees without a driver, and get-driver offers one built for your kernel when one exists.
  • The slow generic driver. A Realtek adapter on cdc_ncm works but drops and tops out lower. Realtek mode moves it to the TV's own r8152 where the kernel has one.

USB 2.0 vs USB 3.0

Many LG TVs, the C2 included, only have USB 2.0 ports. There every adapter tops out around 300 Mbit/s, about three times the built-in port. If your TV has a USB 3.0 port (usually the blue one), plug into that. A USB-C adapter needs a USB-C to USB-A converter on most LG TVs, and those are usually USB 2.0.

Check before you buy

On a rooted TV, LG-USB-2-ETH0's status lists which USB network drivers your kernel has. Its firmware audit lists them model by model, read straight from LG's firmware.

FAQ

Does the brand matter?
Less than the chip. The TV needs a driver for the adapter's chip, and that depends on its kernel. Realtek RTL8153 or RTL8156 and ASIX AX88179 are the safe picks.
Should I buy a 2.5G adapter?
Only if your TV has a USB 3.0 port. On a USB 2.0 port, which many LG TVs have, a gigabit adapter does about as well for less. Both land around 300 Mbit/s.
How do I know which chip an adapter has?
Check the maker's datasheet or reviews, since many listings don't say. On the TV, lsusb shows the ID: 0bda:8153 is Realtek RTL8153, 0bda:8156 is RTL8156, and 0b95:1790 is ASIX AX88179.
Which adapter for an LG C2, C4 or G4?
All three have a Realtek or ASIX driver to use. Tested: an RTL8156 2.5G adapter on a C2, a TP-Link UE306 (AX88179) on a C4, and RTL8153 and AX88179 adapters on G4s. Their ports run at USB 2.0 speed, so a gigabit adapter does as well as a 2.5G one.
My adapter isn't detected. Now what?
The kernel probably has no driver for its chip. LG-USB-2-ETH0's status lists adapters the kernel sees without a driver, get-driver offers a driver built for your kernel when one is known, and test-driver one waiting for testers.
