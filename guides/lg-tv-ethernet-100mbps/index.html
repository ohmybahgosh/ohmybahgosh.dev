LG TV Ethernet stuck at 100 Mbps?

It isn't your cable or your router. LG builds its TVs' wired port as 100 Mbit/s Ethernet. Here's what that means, and the ways around it.

Short answer. The built-in port is 100 Mbit/s, about 95 in practice. On a rooted TV, a USB Ethernet adapter made into the TV's eth0 with LG-USB-2-ETH0 gets about 250 to 330 Mbit/s over USB 2.0, and settings and streaming apps treat it as the real wired connection.

Why it's 100 Mbit/s

LG's TVs use a Fast Ethernet controller for the wired port: 100 Mbit/s, with real throughput around 95. It's a hardware limit, so a gigabit switch, a new Cat6 cable or a faster internet plan won't move it. For most streaming apps that's plenty. It isn't for high-bitrate local files: 4K Blu-ray remuxes over Plex or Jellyfin, and game streaming with Moonlight, can stutter or buffer.

Your options

1. Wi-Fi

On a strong Wi-Fi 5 or Wi-Fi 6 signal, the TV's Wi-Fi can beat the wired port on paper. It's also less steady, and steadiness is what high-bitrate playback needs. Worth a test, especially with the router in the same room.

2. A USB Ethernet adapter, on a stock TV

Plug one in and the TV may detect it, but webOS names it eth1. The TV's settings then show "Not Connected", and some apps, like Paramount+, refuse to play over it. Without root there's no way to change that.

3. A USB Ethernet adapter as eth0, on a rooted TV

LG-USB-2-ETH0 makes the adapter the TV's eth0, the name webOS and its apps expect, and parks the built-in port as eth2. Settings show the wired connection and apps play over the faster link. It runs at every boot, follows the adapter being plugged and unplugged, can keep Wi-Fi as a backup, and undoes cleanly. Where your kernel's driver for the adapter is missing or slow, it can find, check and load one built for your exact kernel.

What you need

  • A rooted LG TV with the Homebrew Channel: see the install guide.
  • A USB Ethernet adapter with a chip your TV has a driver for: see which adapter to buy.
  • Wi-Fi connected while you set it up, as a way back in while the ports switch over.

Then add https://ohmybahgosh.dev/repo to the Homebrew Channel, install LG-USB-2-ETH0 and choose Run at every boot.

Check the link isn't 100 Mbit/s too

If the adapter also shows 100 Mbit/s, it's usually the cable: flat and older cables often carry only two of their four wire pairs. Try a Cat5e or better cable, or another port on the router or switch. LG-USB-2-ETH0's status screen flags a 100 Mbit/s link.

FAQ

Is the LG TV Ethernet port really only 100 Mbit/s?
On LG's TVs, yes: the built-in port is Fast Ethernet, 100 Mbit/s, which works out to about 95 Mbit/s of real traffic. A faster router, switch or cable won't change that. The TV's settings may still say the link is connected at 100.
Will a gigabit or 2.5G USB adapter give me gigabit?
No. Most LG TVs only have USB 2.0 ports, which top out around 300 Mbit/s in practice. That's still about three times the built-in port. On a TV with a USB 3.0 port (usually the blue one), use that for more.
Can I just plug in a USB Ethernet adapter without root?
The TV may see it, but webOS calls it eth1. The settings then show no wired connection, and some streaming apps, like Paramount+, refuse to play over it. Making the adapter the TV's eth0 needs root, which is what LG-USB-2-ETH0 does.
Is Wi-Fi faster than the wired port?
Often, on a good Wi-Fi 5 or Wi-Fi 6 connection close to the router. It's less steady than a cable, though, and that's what causes buffering on high-bitrate streams. Test both with the same file or speed test.
What needs more than 100 Mbit/s?
4K Blu-ray remuxes from Plex, Jellyfin or a local server, which can peak well above 100 Mbit/s, and game streaming like Moonlight at high bitrates. Regular streaming apps use far less.
