~/lg-usb-2-eth0 v2.4

LG-USB-2-ETH0

LG TVs ship with a 100 Mbit/s port. This makes a USB Ethernet adapter the TV's eth0, so settings and streaming apps use the faster wired link. Two to three times the speed, at every boot, from a TV app in the Homebrew Channel, with Wi-Fi as a backup if the cable stops working.

Built like it's your TV.

Because it is. Every project follows the same rules.

01

Look before you touch

Each tool reads your model, webOS version, kernel and free space first, and says what it will do before it does it.

02

Undo is a command

One file in the Homebrew Channel's boot folder, a clean uninstall, and a reboot that always starts clean.

03

Tested on real TVs

Every release runs on a real LG TV before it ships. Before that, test suites stand in for one: a fake /sys, ip and opkg under busybox and dash, and the TV apps run in a headless browser.

04

No accounts, no tracking

Open source and MIT licensed. Bring your own playlist. Nothing phones home except an opt-out anonymous install count in TiviBro.

05

Made for real people

Plain-language READMEs, doctor and report commands, and bug reports you can file from your couch.

AI-assisted. Not AI slop.

A lot of the code in these projects, and on this site, is written with AI coding agents (Claude). I'd rather say so than have you guess. What makes it more than slop is everything around the code.

01

I decide what it does

I design every feature, set the rules the agents work under, and turn down plenty of what they suggest. 17+ years of building software, a day job in data engineering, and pentesting for fun go into every review.

02

Real TVs, every release

Nothing ships until it runs on a real LG TV. TiviBro runs daily on webOS 4, 5, 22 and 25 sets. LG-USB-2-ETH0 is tested on an LG C2 running webOS 25.

03

Tests, not vibes

TiviBro runs lint, unit tests and a test build on every push. LG-USB-2-ETH0 runs 60+ tests under busybox, dash and bash, plus ShellCheck. The Entware installer runs 250+ tests under busybox and dash, plus a headless test of its TV app.

04

The TV finds what the AI misses

The fixes that mattered most came from testing on a real TV, not from the AI: standby putting the adapter back on the slow driver, webOS quietly sending traffic over Wi-Fi instead of the adapter, and a report form opening blank on a phone.

05

Nothing hidden

Commits list me as the author and Claude as the committer, so the AI's part is right there in the git history, and the commits show as Verified. Downloads are checked by SHA-256. Found a problem? Tell me.

before you start

You need a rooted TV.

Everything here runs on LG TVs with the webOS Homebrew Channel. Check whether your firmware can be rooted at cani.rootmy.tv, then turn on SSH in the Homebrew Channel's settings. TiviBro also installs in Developer Mode, no root needed.

Root the TV

Follow webosbrew.org/rooting for your firmware.

SSH in as root

$ ssh root@TV_IP

Pick a tool

Every repo page has a one-line install and a one-line uninstall. Press / to jump.
