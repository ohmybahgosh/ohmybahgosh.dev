LG-USB-2-ETH0

LG TVs ship with a 100 Mbit/s wired port. A gigabit USB Ethernet adapter is two to three times faster on the same TV, even on a USB 2.0 port, but webOS names it eth1 and parts of webOS only look at eth0. So the settings screen says "Not Connected" while the internet works, and some streaming apps refuse to play.

This app makes the adapter the TV's eth0 and parks the built-in port under another name, so webOS and its apps treat the adapter as the TV's wired connection. A small boot hook does it again at every start.

What it fixes

  • Streaming apps that won't play over the adapter, because they only trust eth0.
  • "Not Connected" in the TV's network settings while the adapter is working.
  • The 100 Mbit/s ceiling that makes high-bitrate remuxes, Plex and Moonlight stutter.

In the app

  • Status at a glance: whether the adapter is eth0, its driver, link speed, USB bus, address, Wi-Fi, and any problems found, like a cable that only connects at 100 Mbit/s (usually a cable with only two wire pairs).
  • Switch now, Run at every boot and Follow plug and unplug, as simple on/off switches.
  • Wi-Fi as a backup: if the cable stops working for a minute while Wi-Fi is off, the TV turns Wi-Fi on and joins a network it already knows. Once the cable works again, Wi-Fi goes back off, so traffic returns to the adapter. Off until you turn it on.
  • Faster driver: offered only when a Realtek or ASIX driver is known to work on your TV's exact kernel and an adapter it supports is plugged in. Some TVs have no driver for the chip at all, like 2024 QNED and OLED models for Realtek, or a 2020 UN7300 and a 2025 B5 for ASIX. It downloads, checks and sets up the driver in one go.
  • Block updates harder: on top of the Homebrew Channel's Block system updates, never instead of it. It turns off webOS's own update check, blocks the update servers that block misses, drops traffic to LG's update IP with one firewall rule, and clears cached update details. At every boot, and turning it off undoes only its own changes.
  • Speed test: download, upload and latency through the TV's current connection, against Cloudflare's nearest server.
  • Report from the couch: a QR code opens a compatibility report on your phone with your TV, its chip and built-in USB network drivers, your adapter, speed and the full report already filled in, MAC addresses hidden.
  • Updates through the Homebrew Channel, like any other app from this repository. After an update, the app opens with a short list of what's new.
  • Upgrading from v1? The app walks you through it, step by step, no SSH needed.

Real speeds

  • Built-in port: about 95 Mbit/s.
  • Realtek RTL8153 on the generic driver: about 200 to 255 Mbit/s.
  • Realtek RTL8156 on a 2022 C2, USB 2.0, faster driver: 272 Mbit/s down, 287 up.
  • RTL8153 with the faster driver: about 290 to 330 Mbit/s.
  • ASIX AX88179 on a 2024 G4, the TV's own driver: 292 Mbit/s down, 51 up.
  • ASIX AX88179 on a 2020 UN7300, driver from the app: 228 Mbit/s down, 325 up.

Don't expect a full gigabit: the TV's USB port is the limit (on a C2 at full speed, the CPU was only about a third busy). Two to three times the built-in port is still plenty for 4K remuxes.

Choosing an adapter

The chip matters more than the brand. Realtek RTL8153 (gigabit) and RTL8156 (2.5G) are the most common and work well, especially with the faster driver. ASIX AX88179 works out of the box on many TVs, and where the kernel has no ASIX driver, the app may offer one. Use a USB 3.0 port if your TV has one, but USB 2.0 is fine. The app's status screen shows whether your TV's kernel has a driver for the adapter you plug in.

Safe, and easy to undo

  • Adds one boot hook and one settings file, and only renames network interfaces. No system files are changed, and network settings only if you turn on the Wi-Fi backup.
  • With no adapter plugged in, it changes nothing.
  • A reboot without the hook always starts clean.
  • Turn everything off in the app removes the hook and gives eth0 back to the built-in port. Do that before you remove the app.
