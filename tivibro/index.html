release notes ↓

Skip intro and credits, free

Plex charges for this. TiviBro gives you Skip intro, Skip recap and Skip credits on your movies and episodes, as a button or automatically. The times come from the file's own chapters, or from TheIntroDB and IntroDB, two free community databases. Skip credits brings up Up next, counting down to the next episode. New in alpha 208.

Sports, with live scores

Today's games with live scores and logos from ESPN: your teams first, then everything live, then a row per league from the NFL and college football to the NBA, NHL, MLB, soccer and Formula 1. Each game is matched to the channel of yours that has it, by guide, title or network.

A page for every game

OK on a game: the score in the two teams' colours, the box score by period, each team's leaders, every channel of yours with the game and the division or conference standings. Finals mark the winner. Back from a game you watched from its page lands on the page again.

Your teams' scores while you watch

Watching a movie or another channel while your team plays? Its live score sits in the top corner, several games taking turns, and never over the game on screen. A close finish asks whether to switch, and Hide scores keeps a recorded game spoiler-free.

Multiview, smoother

Add a channel is a small guide over both boxes, the second box reads every channel at its own pace (a steady 29 to 30 pictures a second on a C2), and swapping the sound keeps a picture up.

Opens straight onto anything

Home screens, launchers, scripts and Home Assistant can open TiviBro on a channel, a team's game, the guide or Sports with a tivibro:// link or launch params. Launch API.

More free guides

Samsung TV Plus, Plex and Roku channels get their own guides from i.mjh.nz, like Pluto TV's, matched by id.

Sports in the phone app

A Sports tab in the Android app with the TV's rows of games. Tap one for its box score, leaders and standings, then Watch on TV, Remind me or Follow.

What no other IPTV player does.

TiviBro follows TiviMate's layout on purpose. These are the things we haven't found anywhere else, on any platform.

QR

Set up from your phone

A QR code waits beside the login form. Scan it and the editor opens on your phone, signed in. Type your login with a real keyboard, and the TV moves on by itself.

A tour built from your playlist

Right after you connect, TiviBro counts what your list has and asks only about that: countries, East or West, local cities, event channels, duplicates.

PPV

Reads sports and PPV

"NCAAB 001 | NORTH FLORIDA @ CHARLESTON SOUTHERN" becomes NCAAB 001, with the game in the guide at its real time, in your time zone.

Fails over by itself

Same channel in FHD, HD and SD? When one dies, TiviBro moves to the next copy on its own and tells you.

My teams

Follow your NFL, NBA, NHL, MLB, WNBA and MLS teams, college football and basketball. Their games from every channel gather near the top of the guide.

Logos for every channel

Missing logos filled in from Worldwide TV Logos, tv-logos and iptv-org, matched by guide id, name, country and call sign, in white for dark screens.

A real editor

Clean up 10,000 channels with a keyboard and mouse, then Send to TV. Drafts, undo and version history.

Locked backups

Everything in one AES-256-GCM file. Restore onto a brand-new TV from the very first screen.

REC

A free DVR

Record from the guide to a USB drive on a rooted TV, or to a Windows share or WebDAV on your PC or NAS from any TV. Pause any live channel for hours and move back with a timeline, not only on catch-up channels.

▶

My media, Plex-style

Your own movies and shows from Windows shares, media servers, WebDAV, Google Drive or Dropbox, with TMDB details, a full-screen Up next that counts down to the next episode, and Skip intro and Skip credits as a button or automatically, free.

How it's built.

One install file for every LG TV from 2018 on, and no framework in the way.

JS

Plain JavaScript, no bundler

The TV app is plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript that parses on Chromium 53, the browser engine of webOS 4. No async/await, no optional chaining, no CSS grid. Lint and the tests fail if anything newer slips in.

ES5

A helper on the TV

webOS won't let an app talk to most IPTV servers, so a Node service in the same package relays streams and guides, unzips .xml.gz guides, converts sound the TV can't play and forgives sloppy provider replies. Plain ES5, down to Node 0.12.

4→26

Run on the real engines

The app runs in Chromium 53, 69, 78 and 87, and the helper in Node 0.12 to 20, in the engine tests. Then on real TVs: a 2019 C9, a 2020 NANO, a 2022 32LQ and a C2 on webOS 25.

15K

Fast on slow TVs

Lists stay quick with 15,000 channels, big guides live in the helper or IndexedDB, and a channel change reaches a moving picture in about half a second on TV-speed CPU.

HW

The TV's own decoder

Every stream ends up on the TV's hardware decoder: hls.js and mpegts.js only unpack it, and the TV's player takes Dolby Vision and the codecs the browser refuses.

install

Get it on your TV.

Three ways onto the TV, plus the phone app. The Homebrew Channel route updates itself.

Add the repository

Homebrew Channel, Settings, Add repository:

https://tivibro.app/repo

Want every test build first? Use https://tivibro.app/testing instead. Already added https://ohmybahgosh.dev/repo for LG-USB-2-ETH0, Entware or SSH Doctor? It carries TiviBro's test builds too, so you only need one.

Install

TiviBro appears in the app list. Open it and choose Install. New versions show up as Update.

Scan the QR code

Open TiviBro and scan the first screen with your phone to add your playlist there.

Switch on SSH in the Homebrew Channel, log in as root, and run:

$ curl -fsSL https://tivibro.app/install | sh

Downloads the newest build, checks its SHA-256, installs it and starts TiviBro. Run it again any time to update. Options:

# newest test build
$ curl -fsSL https://tivibro.app/install | sh -s -- --pre
# a specific release
$ curl -fsSL https://tivibro.app/install | sh -s -- --tag v0.6.0-alpha.105

Put your TV in Developer Mode, then build and install from source:

$ git clone https://github.com/ohmybahgosh/TiviBro && cd TiviBro
$ npm install
$ npx ares-setup-device
$ npm run build
$ npm run install:tv
$ npm run launch

The TiviBro app for Android pairs with the TV: a remote, Play on TV from your channel list, volume, the full editor, Movies, Shows and My media to browse, and Turn on over the network and Bluetooth, also as a Quick Settings tile. It updates itself when a new version is out. Pair it on the TV under Settings > Phone app and web editor.

faq

Questions.

Was this written by AI?
A lot of it, with AI coding agents (Claude), and I design, review and test every change. Lint, unit tests and a test build run on every push, and TiviBro runs every day on real LG TVs from webOS 4 to webOS 25 before a build goes out. Commits list me as the author and Claude as the committer, so it's all in the open. How these are built.
Do I need a rooted TV?
No. Developer Mode works today. A rooted TV with the Homebrew Channel is the easiest route, with updates from the TV itself.
Which playlists does it support?
Xtream Codes, M3U with an XMLTV guide, or a Stalker portal (with its serial number when the portal asks for one). MPEG-DASH channels play too. Got one long get.php link from your provider? Paste it under Full link and TiviBro fills in the server, username and password.
Does it track me?
No account, no ads, no tracking. There's an anonymous daily count of installs by version, which you can switch off.
Found a bug?
On the TV, Settings > System > Report a problem. Scan the QR code and a GitHub issue opens on your phone, filled in with the TV's version, model and log, with logins and server names taken out.
