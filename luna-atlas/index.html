The webOS Luna bus,
mapped.

Every LG webOS TV ships luna-send, the command that talks to the Luna Service Bus: power, apps, inputs, picture and sound, Wi-Fi, notifications and hundreds more. There has never been a complete list of what you can say to it. This is that list: 8,125 methods across 501 services, each with its access level, where it came from, and a ready-to-run command.

man luna-send

One bus. Every service.

The Luna Service Bus is the internal message bus every webOS system service listens on. On a rooted TV or in Developer Mode, luna-send is how you drive any of it from a shell: show a toast, switch inputs, read the model, change a picture setting, or ask a service what it can do.

Methods marked sub also stream updates: add "subscribe": true and drop -n 1 to keep listening.

$ luna-send -n 1 -f luna://com.webos.notification/createToast '{"message":"Hello from luna-send"}'
⚠

This drives a real TV

Many calls change settings, inputs, power or the panel, and a few can factory reset, wipe data or brick the set. Those are flagged and listed in dangerous.md. Running one is exactly as safe as typing the same luna-send yourself.

Honest about what's known

Every method carries a confidence flag, and so does every service description. Inferred entries say so, and their parameters are left blank rather than guessed.

The catalog is the main part, and it is still growing. The repo also has a luna-atlas.sh script that reads the same catalog. It is an early preview and not ready for real use yet: the focus is documenting every Luna call first. Method names are solid, parameters are still being filled in, and categories may move.

What it isn't. It maps the services and methods that exist on a TV you own, private and dangerous ones included. It doesn't publish exploits: no region or country lock bypasses, no DRM workarounds, and no way around LG's store sign-in or app signing.

faq

Questions.

Do I need a rooted TV?
To run most of these, yes, or Developer Mode for some. luna-send is on every webOS TV, but private and root methods only answer a privileged caller. Reading the catalog needs nothing at all.
What does "inferred" mean?
The method exists on a real TV, but LG publishes nothing about it. Its description is worked out from the method name alone, and its parameters are left empty instead of guessed. Treat it as a pointer, not documentation.
Is it safe to try methods?
Reading methods (get…, list…, status calls) usually are. Anything that sets, resets, deletes or reboots can change the TV, and the ones that can do real damage are flagged dangerous. There's no warranty, so know what a call does before you send it.
Does it include bypasses or exploits?
No. It documents what exists on the Luna bus so you can understand and control a TV you own. Bypassing region or country locks, DRM, or LG's store sign-in and app signing is out of scope.
Can I use the data in my own project?
Yes. It's MIT licensed, and the full catalog is plain JSON and JSONL in the repo. This page reads the same catalog.json straight from the repo, so it's current as soon as the repo is.
Was this written by AI?
It's built with AI coding agents (Claude), directed, reviewed and checked by me against a real LG C2. The confidence flags exist so nothing a model guessed passes for documentation. How these are built.
